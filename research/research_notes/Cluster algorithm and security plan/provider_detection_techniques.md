# Spam and Phishing Detection Techniques at Major Providers and Vendors, and Whether a Metadata-Only Browser Extension Can Reproduce Them

Research date: 2026-10-20. Method note: this sandbox's egress proxy blocked direct fetches of learn.microsoft.com, support.google.com, security.googleblog.com, developers.google.com and RDAP servers. Findings from those domains come from search-engine excerpts of the primary pages (URLs cited are the primary pages). The Sublime Security rules repo was cloned and read directly (commit dated 2026-10-19), so those findings are first-hand. Items I could not verify against a primary source are labelled as such.

## Google / Gmail defenses, bulk-sender rules, and Gmail-visible headers

### Takeaway
Gmail's core filtering (ML text classifiers such as RETVec, Safe Browsing, attachment scanning and sandboxing, sender reputation) runs server-side before delivery and is not exposed as a readable score. A client can only see the *results* Gmail writes into headers (Authentication-Results, ARC-Authentication-Results, Received-SPF), the presence of the spam label, and the sender-compliance artifacts that the 2024 bulk-sender rules made mandatory (DKIM, DMARC, List-Unsubscribe / List-Unsubscribe-Post). Those artifacts are good client-side features.

### Cited Findings
- Google says its ML models, together with other protections, block more than 99.9% of spam, phishing and malware from reaching Gmail inboxes. This is Google's own number and has not been independently measured. — [Google Workspace blog](https://workspace.google.com/blog/product-announcements/bringing-businesses-more-proactive-phishing-protections-and-data-controls-in-g-suite)
- RETVec (Resilient and Efficient Text Vectorizer, open-sourced Nov 2023) is a lightweight word-embedding/vectorizer of about 200k parameters. It is built to resist homoglyphs, invisible characters, LEET substitutions and keyword stuffing. In Gmail it raised the spam detection rate over baseline by 38%, cut the false-positive rate by 19.4%, and cut TPU usage by 83%. — [Google Security Blog, Nov 2023](https://security.googleblog.com/2023/11/improving-text-classification.html); corroborated by [The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2023/11/google-unveils-retvec-gmails-new.html) and [9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/2023/12/04/gmail-spam-detection-retvec/)
- Workspace "Advanced phishing and malware protection" lets admins set warning banners, spam, or quarantine for unusual attachment types, anomalous attachments in Google Groups, spoofing of the domain or employee names, and unauthenticated mail. Google scans all messages for malware whether or not attachment-security settings are on. Warning banners appear only in Gmail web; third-party clients do not show them. — [Google Workspace Admin Help: Advanced phishing and malware protection](https://knowledge.workspace.google.com/admin/gmail/advanced/advanced-phishing-and-malware-protection)
- Sources conflict on whether these advanced protections are on by default. Google's blog describes them as default-on; a third-party Workspace guide says the built-in protections are off by default. — [Google Workspace blog](https://workspace.google.com/blog/product-announcements/bringing-businesses-more-proactive-phishing-protections-and-data-controls-in-g-suite) vs [Adaptive Security guide](https://www.adaptivesecurity.com/blog/email-security-for-google-workspace-the-complete-guide)
- Workspace "security sandbox" (attachment detonation) covers Office documents, executables, PDFs and archives (ZIP/RAR). This comes from a vendor blog that attributes it to Google's docs; I did not see Google's own text. — [Adaptive Security](https://www.adaptivesecurity.com/blog/email-security-for-google-workspace-the-complete-guide)
- Bulk-sender requirements (announced Oct 2023, phased enforcement from Feb 2024). A bulk sender is anyone sending ≥5,000 messages/day to Gmail accounts. Requirements are SPF **and** DKIM, DMARC (at least p=none) aligned with the From domain, valid forward/reverse DNS, TLS, and RFC 5322 compliance. Senders must not impersonate Gmail From addresses. Marketing/promotional mail must carry one-click unsubscribe (RFC 8058) plus a visible body link, and unsubscribes must be honoured within 2 days. Transactional mail is exempt from one-click unsubscribe. The user-reported spam rate in Postmaster Tools must stay below 0.3%. Starting June 2024, senders above 0.3% are ineligible for mitigation, and they become eligible again after 7 consecutive days below 0.3%. Rates above 0.1% already hurt inbox placement. — [Google Workspace Admin Help: Email sender guidelines FAQ](https://support.google.com/a/answer/14229414); [Email sender guidelines](https://support.google.com/a/answer/81126); [TechSpot](https://techspot.com/news/100374-google-new-spam-rules-ensures-senders-5000-daily.html)
- RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe means the sender includes `List-Unsubscribe: <https://...>` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click`. Both header fields must be covered by a valid DKIM signature so the receiver can trust them. — [RFC 8058](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc8058)
- BIMI logos in Gmail require DMARC at enforcement (p=quarantine or p=reject; some sources add pct=100). The blue verified checkmark needs a VMC, which requires a registered trademark. Sources disagree on what a CMC (Common Mark Certificate) gets you: one says Google accepts CMCs for logo display, another says a CMC shows the logo but not the checkmark. — [Encryption Consulting](https://www.encryptionconsulting.com/bimi-with-vmc-and-cmc/); [Suped](https://www.suped.com/knowledge/email-deliverability/technical/do-i-need-a-vmc-for-bimi-to-work-with-google-and-gmail) (secondary; Google's BIMI help page not fetched)
- Gmail fails open on SPF temperror. Mail spoofing a domain that has no MX/SPF record can land in the Gmail inbox, with header `Received-SPF: temperror (google.com: error in processing during lookup of ...)` and `Authentication-Results: mx.google.com; spf=temperror`. O365 fails closed to spam on the same case. Sublime ships a rule matching this header. — [sublime-rules: headers_spf_temp_error.yml](https://github.com/sublime-security/sublime-rules/blob/main/detection-rules/headers_spf_temp_error.yml)
- Gmail API `format=METADATA` returns "only email message ID, labels, and email headers", and `metadataHeaders` filters which headers come back. `format` cannot be used with the narrow `gmail.metadata` scope. — [Gmail API Format reference](https://developers.google.com/workspace/gmail/api/reference/rest/v1/Format); [users.threads.get](https://developers.google.cn/gmail/api/v1/reference/users/threads/get)
- Header semantics. `Authentication-Results` records per-method verdicts (spf=, dkim=, dmarc=, with header.from / smtp.mailfrom / header.d properties) — [RFC 8601](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc8601). `ARC-Authentication-Results` (AAR) is an intermediary's copy of the auth results it saw, sealed with ARC-Seal / ARC-Message-Signature so a later hop can trust them after forwarding — [RFC 8617](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc8617). DMARC passes when SPF or DKIM passes **and** the authenticated domain aligns with the RFC5322.From domain (relaxed = same organizational domain, strict = exact match) — [RFC 7489](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc7489). SPF — [RFC 7208](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc7208). DKIM — [RFC 6376](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc6376).
- I found no primary documentation of `X-Gm-Message-State` or `X-Google-Smtp-Source`. They are undocumented Google-internal values (added on Google-originated or -relayed mail) and carry no verdict anyone outside Google can interpret. — search found only generic guides, e.g. [Mailflow Authority](https://mailflowauthority.com/email-infrastructure/email-headers-gmail)
- Gemini-in-Gmail is an attack surface as well as a defense. In July 2025, 0DIN researchers showed hidden-text prompt injection making Gemini's email summary display a fake Google security warning, with no links or attachments, so it bypasses link and attachment filters. Google described layered ML defenses against prompt injection in June 2025. — [0DIN](https://0din.ai/blog/phishing-for-gemini); [BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/google-gemini-flaw-hijacks-email-summaries-for-phishing/)

### Inferences
- In a Gmail-hosted mailbox, the most useful client-readable verdict is the `Authentication-Results: mx.google.com; spf=…; dkim=…; dmarc=…` line, plus the ARC chain on forwarded or mailing-list mail. Gmail writes no numeric spam score like Microsoft's SCL. The SPAM label, if the extension can see it, is the only Gmail spam verdict.
- Bulk-sender compliance features are cheap, metadata-only discriminators for legitimate bulk mail: DKIM d= aligned with From, DMARC present, List-Unsubscribe-Post=One-Click present, and List-Unsubscribe under DKIM `h=`. Their absence in "marketing-looking" mail from a high-volume ESP is suspicious. Their presence does not prove the mail is wanted.
- RETVec is open source (TensorFlow/TF.js-compatible), so an equivalent small text model could in principle run in the browser on the subject line without body opt-in. This is inferred from the ~200k-parameter size; I did not benchmark it.
- Whether the Gmail web DOM exposes headers without the API is not established here. Reliable header access almost certainly needs the Gmail API (OAuth) or the "Show original" raw view.

### Gaps
- Could not fetch Google's own pages directly. The exact wording and dates of 2025–2026 enforcement escalations (e.g., Google moving to rejecting non-compliant mail in late 2025) are unverified.
- No public Google figure for Gemini/LLM-based spam classifiers in production Gmail as of 2026.
- I could not confirm from Google docs whether `Authentication-Results`/`ARC-*` are returned when requested via `metadataHeaders` under the `gmail.metadata` scope. They are standard headers and probably are, but this should be tested.
- Safe Browsing's use inside Gmail (link scanning at delivery and click) is widely described, but I did not retrieve a primary source in this session.

## Microsoft Defender for Office 365 / EOP: spoof intelligence, compauth, impersonation, SCL/BCL/PCL headers, Safe Links, Safe Attachments, first-contact tip

### Takeaway
Microsoft writes rich, documented verdicts into the message headers: X-Forefront-Antispam-Report (SCL, SFV, CAT, SFTY, IPV, CIP, CTRY, SRV:BULK), X-Microsoft-Antispam (BCL, PCL), and Authentication-Results with compauth and reason codes. These are standard internet headers, and Microsoft Graph exposes them through the `internetMessageHeaders` property. A client like Cluster could therefore read Microsoft's own spam, bulk, phish, spoof and impersonation verdicts on M365 mailboxes rather than re-deriving them. Safe Links and Safe Attachments detonation are server/network functions and cannot be reproduced. Their *traces* (rewritten safelinks.protection.outlook.com URLs, which need the body) are visible.

### Cited Findings
- EOP scans all inbound messages in every Microsoft 365 org and writes results to `X-Forefront-Antispam-Report`, `X-Microsoft-Antispam` and `Authentication-results`. Fields are semicolon-separated `NAME:VALUE` pairs (e.g., `SFV:NSPM`). — [Microsoft Learn: Anti-spam message headers](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/defender-office-365/message-headers-eop-mdo)
- `SCL` is the spam confidence level, -1 to 9 (-1 = bypass/trusted; 5–6 = spam; 9 = high-confidence spam, per Microsoft's training table). `SFV` is the spam filter verdict, e.g. `SFV:SPM` = marked spam, `SFV:NSPM` = not spam; other documented values include SKN, SKS, SKA, SKB, SKI, SKQ, SFE, BLK. `SRV:BULK` means the message was identified as bulk by BCL threshold; with MarkAsSpamBulkMail on (default) it gets SCL 6. — [Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/defender-office-365/message-headers-eop-mdo); [TechNet archive](https://technet.microsoft.com/en-us/library/dn205071.aspx); [Learn training: SCL](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/training/modules/manage-anti-malware-anti-spam-policies/2-examine-message-headers-spam-confidence-levels)
- `CAT` is the category of protection policy applied. Values include PHSH (phishing), HPHSH/HPHISH (high-confidence phish), SPOOF, UIMP (user impersonation), DIMP (domain impersonation), GIMP (mailbox-intelligence impersonation), BIMP (brand impersonation), SAP (Safe Attachments), BULK, SPM, HSPM, MALW, AMP, OSPM, NONE. BIMP, SAP and UIMP apply only with Defender for Office 365. An older Tech Community post uses "PHISH"; current Learn uses PHSH. — [Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/defender-office-365/message-headers-eop-mdo); [Tech Community](https://techcommunity.microsoft.com/blog/microsoftdefenderforoffice365blog/email-protection-basics-in-microsoft-365-spam--phish/3555712)
- `X-Microsoft-Antispam` carries `BCL` (bulk complaint level, 0–9; higher = more complaint-generating bulk) and `PCL` (phishing confidence level). PCL's exact definition was not in the excerpts I could see. Third-party sites describe it as legacy phishing confidence. — [Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/defender-office-365/message-headers-eop-mdo); [Suped](https://www.suped.com/learn/email-deliverability/what-do-microsoft-email-headers-reveal-about-spam-classification)
- First-contact safety tip: shown to recipients who rarely or never get mail from the sender, available in all cloud mailboxes, and independent of spoof/impersonation settings. It is signalled by `SFTY:9.25` in X-Forefront-Antispam-Report. Other SFTY sub-codes (e.g., 9.11, 9.22) mark spoof / intra-org sub-classifications. — [Microsoft Learn: Anti-phishing policies](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/defender-office-365/anti-phishing-policies-about); [Anti-spoofing protection](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/defender-office-365/anti-phishing-protection-spoofing-about)
- Composite authentication (`compauth=pass|fail|softpass|none reason=NNN`) combines SPF, DKIM, DMARC, ARC and proprietary signals (spoof intelligence, sender reputation, history). Reason 000 = explicit failure (DMARC fail with p=quarantine/reject). Reason 001 = implicit failure: the domain has no auth records or a weak policy, and spoof intelligence decides. CAT:SPOOF with reason=000 indicates DMARC/anti-spoofing enforcement, not the content filter. — [Microsoft Learn: Troubleshoot anti-spam policies](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/defender-office-365/anti-spam-policies-troubleshooting); [Message headers](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/defender-office-365/message-headers-eop-mdo)
- Microsoft's own pages give conflicting definitions of some reason codes. On 002, the headers page says the org has prohibited the sender/domain pair from spoofing, while the ARC page says an explicit SPF/DKIM bypass prevented evaluation. On 010, one page says DMARC failed on an accepted domain (intra-org spoof), another says the message was excluded from DMARC (e.g., sent to self). 1xx = pass codes (101 = DKIM signed by From domain, 102 = SPF aligned), 2xx = softpass, 130 = ARC override by a trusted ARC sealer, 6xx = intra-org spoofing. Third parties say pass covers 1xx/7xx and fail covers 000/001/002/010/6xx. — [Microsoft Learn: Configure trusted ARC sealers](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/defender-office-365/email-authentication-arc-configure); [Anti-spoofing protection](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/defender-office-365/anti-phishing-protection-spoofing-about); [DMARCTrust (secondary)](https://www.dmarctrust.com/troubleshooting/compauth-fail-reason-codes)
- Impersonation protection (Defender for Office 365) covers user impersonation (protected senders list), domain impersonation (protected/owned domains), and mailbox intelligence. Mailbox intelligence uses each user's communication history to tell normal contacts from impersonators. In preset Standard/Strict policies, all recipients automatically get mailbox-intelligence impersonation protection; Standard moves flagged mail to Junk, Strict quarantines it. — [Microsoft Learn: Preset security policies](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/defender-office-365/preset-security-policies); [Anti-phishing policies](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/defender-office-365/anti-phishing-policies-about)
- Safe Links rewrites URLs and checks them at time of click. Safe Attachments detonates attachments in a sandbox before delivery. The Built-in protection preset applies both to recipients not covered by other policies. (The detonation and time-of-click specifics come from secondary sources; I did not retrieve the dedicated Learn pages.) — [Microsoft Learn: Recommended settings](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/defender-office-365/recommended-settings-for-eop-and-office365); [Solving Microsoft 365 (secondary)](https://www.solvingmicrosoft365.com/guides/defender-for-office-365-explained)
- Reading these via Graph works by requesting a message with `$select=internetMessageHeaders` and iterating the name/value array for `X-Forefront-Antispam-Report`. — [Microsoft Q&A](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/answers/questions/1397679/currently-using-microsoft-graph-wanted-to-know-whe); resource reference [internetMessageHeader](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/graph/api/resources/internetmessageheader)
- Sublime's open rules read Microsoft's verdicts directly from the hop's parsed Authentication-Results, e.g. `.authentication_results.compauth.verdict not in ("pass","softpass")` combined with `dmarc == "none"` and `spf == "none"` to find spoofable internal domains. — [sublime-rules: spoofable_internal_domain_suspicious_signals.yml](https://github.com/sublime-security/sublime-rules/blob/main/detection-rules/spoofable_internal_domain_suspicious_signals.yml)

### Inferences
- **Can Cluster read them?** Yes, for M365/Outlook.com mailboxes accessed through Graph with Mail.Read (or Mail.ReadBasic? see gaps), assuming `internetMessageHeaders` is returned. The headers are standard transport headers stamped on inbound mail, so SCL, SFV, CAT, BCL, SFTY and compauth can be parsed with a regex. This gives Microsoft's spam, bulk, phish, spoof and impersonation verdicts for free, plus first-contact (SFTY:9.25).
- Treat these headers as trustworthy only if stamped by the recipient's own tenant, i.e. at the top of the header list or on the final inbound hop. An attacker can forge an `X-Forefront-Antispam-Report` lower in the message; Microsoft itself relies on position and stripping.
- The same headers also appear on mail that passed *through* Microsoft before reaching Gmail (e.g., a sender on M365). There they describe the sender side and should be weighted lower or ignored.

### Gaps
- I could not confirm from Graph documentation whether `internetMessageHeaders` is returned by `Mail.ReadBasic` (which by design excludes body and attachments) or only by `Mail.Read`. I also could not confirm whether Graph returns every header or truncates/filters (documentation describes it as "the collection of message headers… defined by RFC5322"). Test this on a live tenant.
- The full current SFV and SFTY value tables and the exact PCL definition were not fully visible. The Learn page should be consulted directly.
- The meaning of reason code 905 was not found in any source.

## Email-security vendors: Abnormal, Proofpoint, Mimecast, Barracuda, Cloudflare, Cisco Talos, Sublime, Material, IRONSCALES

### Takeaway
Commercial vendors differentiate on three things a browser extension can only approximate: (1) cross-customer intelligence (Abnormal VendorBase, IRONSCALES crowd reports, Proofpoint's NexusAI telemetry, Talos reputation, Cloudflare's crawling of pre-attack infrastructure); (2) per-user/org relationship baselines built from full mailbox history; and (3) network-side detonation and click-time URL checks. Sublime Security's open MQL rules are the best public, concrete evidence of which header/sender heuristics production systems actually use. Of 1,335 rules, roughly 60 need no body, attachment, ML, WHOIS or sender-profile lookups at all.

### Cited Findings
- **Abnormal Security.** Behavioral AI baselines normal employee–vendor communication (contacts, typical financial requests, timing) and flags anomalies that indicate account compromise. It analyses identity attributes including reply-to changes and mail-forwarding rules. VendorBase is a federated, cross-customer knowledge base: when a vendor account is flagged as compromised anywhere, its mail is flagged as suspicious for all customers. It tracks vendor names, risk levels, contacts and geolocations. Abnormal notes that VEC from a genuinely compromised vendor account passes all authentication checks, and it recommends out-of-band payment verification alongside email detection. These are vendor marketing pages; there is no technical detail on the models. — [Abnormal: VEC](https://abnormal.ai/solutions/vendor-email-compromise); [VendorBase](https://abnormal.ai/resources/vendorbase-security-email-supply-chain); [Vendor impersonation detection](https://abnormal.ai/learning/vendor-impersonation-fraud-detection); [Manufacturing](https://abnormal.ai/solutions/industry/manufacturing)
- **Proofpoint TAP / URL Defense.** Rewrites every URL in delivered mail, predictively sandboxes suspicious URLs before delivery, and re-checks and sandboxes them in real time at click. Attachments are sandboxed. — [Proofpoint TAP datasheet](https://www.proofpoint.com/sites/default/files/data-sheets/pfpt-us-ds-targeted-attack-protection.pdf). NexusAI is described as Proofpoint's ML/threat-intel platform trained on telemetry from 230,000+ organisations. That figure comes from a review site and was not verified with Proofpoint. — [Technologymatch](https://technologymatch.com/blog/proofpoint-vs-mimecast-vs-abnormal-security-email-security-comparison)
- **Cloudflare Email Security (ex-Area 1).** Threat-focused web crawlers scan the internet for attacker infrastructure and campaigns still under construction, and dynamically analyse suspicious pages and payloads. Detection covers new, lookalike and "proximity" domains used to get around SPF/DKIM/DMARC, plus NLU/NLP, social-graph analysis and image recognition. Cloudflare claims it detects malicious sites and payloads 24 days before attacks launch on average (vendor claim). Uncertain links are opened in a headless browser. — [Cloudflare blog: Replace your email gateway with Area 1](https://blog.cloudflare.com/replace-your-email-gateway-with-area-1/); [Area 1 + M365 brief](https://cf-assets.www.cloudflare.com/slt3lc6tev37/3fbAo1KCH0vA1uQjMf0NuT/a07469bb32a15445c459a60a615c2ef8/Enhance_Microsoft_365_Email_Defenses_with_Cloudflare_Area_1.pdf); [Cloudflare DLP + Area 1 blog](https://blog.cloudflare.com/dlp-area1-to-protect-data-in-email/)
- **IRONSCALES.** API-based, in-mailbox product for M365 and Google Workspace. One-click employee phishing reports feed detection across its customer base (self-reported 17,000+ organisations, from a review site). — [Expert Insights](https://expertinsights.com/email-security/top-phishing-protection-solutions)
- **Barracuda Impersonation Protection, Mimecast, Cisco Talos/Secure Email, Material Security.** I did not retrieve primary technical material in this session (see Gaps).
- **Sublime Security open rules (first-hand, cloned 2026-10-20, 1,335 rules in `detection-rules/`).** Function usage across rules:
  - 507 rules reference `headers.auth_summary` (SPF/DMARC pass summaries).
  - 301 use `profile.by_sender*()` (prevalence new/outlier/common, solicited, days_known, any_messages_benign/malicious). This is a per-org sender history store.
  - 380 use `ml.nlu_classifier` (intent such as bec, cred_theft, advance_fee; topics).
  - 109 use WHOIS domain age (`network.whois(...).days_old`).
  - 137 use `ml.link_analysis` (live page crawl).
  - 74 use Levenshtein edit distance (lookalikes).
  - 19 scan QR codes.
  - About 60–65 use none of body, attachments, ML, WHOIS or profile. Examples: `punycode_sender_domain.yml` (`sender.email.domain.domain` contains `xn--`), `disposable_sender_unsolicited.yml`, `sender_freemail_replyto_different_freemail.yml`, `headers_spf_temp_error.yml`, `observed_malicious_sender_domains.yml` / `observed_malicious_reply_to_domains.yml` / `observed_malicious_headers_ips_spamhaus_drop.yml` (static IOC lists), `evasion_variation_selectors_subject.yml`, `impersonation_apple.yml` (display name within Levenshtein ≤2 of "apple developer" while the domain is not apple.com and the sender is not in `$recipient_emails`), `headers_invalid_ios_build.yml`, `headers_zimbra_mailer_unsupported_os_versions.yml`, `sender_local_part_built_from_recipient.yml`. — [sublime-rules repo](https://github.com/sublime-security/sublime-rules/tree/main/detection-rules)
- Concrete Sublime header/sender rules relevant to Cluster:
  - *Lookalike sender domain*: `strings.ilevenshtein(sender.email.domain.domain, <org_domain>) == 1`, SLD length > 3, sender prevalence new/outlier and unsolicited. High-trust domains are negated only when DMARC passes. — [lookalike_sender_domain.yml](https://github.com/sublime-security/sublime-rules/blob/main/detection-rules/lookalike_sender_domain.yml)
  - *New sender domain*: `network.whois(sender.email.domain).days_old <= 10` and `not profile.by_sender().solicited`. — [sender_new_from_domain_first_time_sender.yml](https://github.com/sublime-security/sublime-rules/blob/main/detection-rules/sender_new_from_domain_first_time_sender.yml)
  - *BEC reply-to/return-path mismatch*: a non-freemail sender whose return-path or all reply-to addresses are freemail, plus NLU BEC intent high. It negates Gmail auto-forward return paths (`+caf_=`), listservs (List-Unsubscribe present and "via" in the display name), and real replies (References / In-Reply-To present). — [headers_freemail_replyto_returnpath_mismatch.yml](https://github.com/sublime-security/sublime-rules/blob/main/detection-rules/headers_freemail_replyto_returnpath_mismatch.yml)
  - *Invalid recipient domain + reply-to mismatch + new sender* (pure headers plus sender history). — [headers_invalid_rcpt_reply-to_mismatch_new.yml](https://github.com/sublime-security/sublime-rules/blob/main/detection-rules/headers_invalid_rcpt_reply-to_mismatch_new.yml)
  - *VIP impersonation*: display name equals a name on the synced `$org_vips` list from a never-seen sender. It negates personal↔work mail where the sender display name equals the mailbox's own, and postmaster/mailer-daemon bounces. — [vip_impersonation.yml](https://github.com/sublime-security/sublime-rules/blob/main/detection-rules/vip_impersonation.yml)
  - *Vendor thread hijack*: `subject.is_reply`, a sender root domain not previously seen, Levenshtein 1–2 from a known `$sender_domains` entry, not present in any previous thread participant, BEC intent and financial topic, and domain prevalence new or known < 3 days. — [vendor_impersonation_thread_hijack.yml](https://github.com/sublime-security/sublime-rules/blob/main/detection-rules/vendor_impersonation_thread_hijack.yml)
  - *Display-name keyword lure*: a Gmail sender whose display name contains ≥2 of ~30 urgency/contact words ("urgent", "kindly", "asap", "verify", day or month names, …). — [sender_suspicious_display_name_from_gmail_domain.yml](https://github.com/sublime-security/sublime-rules/blob/main/detection-rules/sender_suspicious_display_name_from_gmail_domain.yml)
  - *X-Source-Auth mismatch*: a cPanel/Exim `X-Source-Auth` value that differs from the From address, with reply-to domain ≠ sender domain. — [headers_x_source_auth_mismatch_bec.yml](https://github.com/sublime-security/sublime-rules/blob/main/detection-rules/headers_x_source_auth_mismatch_bec.yml)
  - Other header-family rules, by filename: `headers_anonymousfox.yml`, `headers_outlook_express.yml`, `headers_php_mailer_with_common_phishing_attachments.yml`, `headers_sendgrid_onmicrosoft.yml`, `headers_system_account_spoof.yml`, `headers_reply_to_wildcard_sender.yml`, `sender_long_local_part.yml`, `sender_local_part_contains_ip.yml`, `suspicious_sender_display_name_procedurally_generated_blob.yml`, `spoofing_hiding_outlook_headers_self_sender.yml`, `self_sender_cred_theft_auth_failures.yml`.

### Inferences
- Sublime's design pattern is worth copying: cheap structural/header predicates combined with a **sender-history gate** (`prevalence == new`, `not solicited`, i.e. the user has never written to this sender) and explicit **negations** for known false-positive sources (auto-forwards, listservs, replies, bounces, high-trust domains that pass DMARC). An extension can keep its own local `profile.by_sender` equivalent in IndexedDB from mailbox history: first-seen date, count, and whether the user ever sent to the address.
- Cross-customer intelligence (VendorBase, crowd reports, Talos) is a server feature by definition. The closest client-side substitute is shipping static IOC/allow lists (Tranco top-1M, free-mail and disposable providers, URL shorteners, free file hosts), which is what Sublime does with `$free_email_providers`, `$tranco_1m`, `$disposable_email_providers` and similar lists.

### Gaps
- No primary technical documents retrieved for Barracuda Impersonation Protection, Mimecast (e.g., its impersonation/"similarity" checks), Cisco Talos / Secure Email (SenderBase reputation), or Material Security. The reviewer claims (NexusAI's 230k orgs, IRONSCALES' 17k orgs) are unverified.
- No vendor publishes accuracy or false-positive rates in a reproducible way. All performance numbers found are vendor-reported.

## Technique catalogue and client-side feasibility (metadata-only extension: headers, sender, subject, date, size; body opt-in; no server)

### Takeaway
About half of the catalogued techniques can be reproduced client-side from metadata: auth parsing and alignment, provider verdict parsing, reply-to/return-path mismatch, display-name spoofing, lookalike/homoglyph/punycode domains, first-time-sender baselines, bulk-header compliance, header anomalies, and subject-level text classification. Domain age (RDAP) is feasible from an extension without a server of our own, but it does make network calls to public registries. Body opt-in unlocks NLP intent, link/QR features and thread-hijack checks. Sandbox detonation, time-of-click URL rewriting, crawling, cross-customer graphs and the reporting loop all need servers.

### Cited Findings
- Header-only ML in academic work: a Random Forest on header-derived features (sender domain, subject length, urgency terms, URL counts, transmission time; Enron vs Nazario corpora) reported 93.6% accuracy, 0.94 precision/recall and ROC-AUC 0.967. Seen via an aggregator; verify against the original. The corpus is dated, and the Enron vs Nazario split is known to be easy. — [Academia.edu: Detecting Phishing Emails Using ML Based on Email Header Features](https://www.academia.edu/144536447/Detecting_Phishing_Emails_Using_Machine_Learning_Based_on_Email_Header_Features)
- Header-based anomaly detection with ML is explored in [arXiv 2203.10408](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.10408). LLM-based phishing detection from full emails (ChatSpamDetector) is in [arXiv 2402.18093](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.18093). A patent notes that only the Received hop added by the recipient's own MTA is trustworthy, because earlier "Received: from" lines can be forged. — [USPTO 11595435](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/11595435)
- RDAP from a browser: RFC 7480 recommends `Access-Control-Allow-Origin: *`, and an IETF deployment draft proposes making it MUST. ICANN's conformance notes say some RDAP servers send the header only when an `Origin` header is present, and that a missing header blocks in-browser clients. rdap.org is a redirector to the authoritative registry, so CORS must also hold at the redirect target. — [ICANN RDAP conformance failures (Dec 2024)](https://itp.cdn.icann.org/en/files/contracted-parties-communications/rdap-conformance-failures-19dec24-en.pdf); [IETF draft-blanchet-regext-rdap-deployfindings](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/draft-blanchet-regext-rdap-deployfindings/01/); [RFC 7480](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc7480); [xevion.dev RDAP client notes](https://xevion.dev/projects/rdap)

### Inferences
- Recommended client-side core, in priority order: (4) read the provider's own verdicts; (1–3) auth parse and alignment with ARC; (13) local sender-history gate; (16) reply-to mismatch; (12) display-name/VIP spoof; (10–11) lookalike/homoglyph/punycode; (17) header-only thread-hijack check; (6) bulk compliance for newsletter clustering; (18) Sublime-derived header fingerprints. Each should carry explicit FP negations as Sublime does.
- RDAP domain age is the main "network but no own server" feature. In an MV3 extension, a background service worker with host_permissions for the RDAP hosts is not subject to page CORS, which works around the inconsistent CORS behaviour ICANN noted. This is inferred from Chrome extension behaviour, not tested here. It does leak queried domains to registries, so it needs disclosure or opt-in.
- Header-only ML accuracy figures (~93–96%) come from old, easy corpora and should not be taken as real-world performance. Expect a precision/recall trade-off dominated by the sender-history gate.

### Gaps

- Could not retrieve dnstwist's documentation in-session. Its permutation classes (addition, bitsquatting, homoglyph, hyphenation, insertion, omission, repetition, replacement, subdomain, transposition, vowel-swap, various TLD swaps) are recalled from general knowledge, not cited.
- No recent (2024–2026) peer-reviewed benchmark of header-only phishing detection on modern corpora was found.
