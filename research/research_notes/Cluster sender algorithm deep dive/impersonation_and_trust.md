# Impersonation and trust: how providers and vendors stop people being fooled, and what works header-only for one household mailbox

Researched 2026-10-16. Builds on `research/2026-10-11-how-cluster-solves-it.md` (Ho 2017/2019, Defender mailbox intelligence basics, Agari H2 2020 split, Boykin 2004 are NOT repeated here). Labels: **verified** = read on the linked page or in the linked paper's text; **vendor** = vendor-reported, not independently checked; **secondary** = press/blog coverage of a primary I did not read; **inference** = my reasoning. Dates on older facts.

Method note: `support.google.com` refused automated fetches, so Gmail consumer-help claims rely on Google Workspace admin docs, Google blogs and secondary coverage. Sublime rules were pulled with `gh api` from `sublime-security/sublime-rules` (main, 1,332 detection rules on 2026-10-16). ## 1. Consumer mailbox providers: what warnings exist and what triggers them

### Takeaway
Consumer providers warn mostly on **authentication failure** (SPF/DKIM/DMARC) and **global reputation**, and badge **verified brands** (BIMI). None of the consumer tiers I could verify warns on "a known contact's name from a new address". That check exists only in paid enterprise tiers (Microsoft Defender, Google Workspace admin settings). Microsoft is the one provider whose consumer-visible signals (`compauth`, `?`, `via`, first-contact tip) are documented down to the header, which makes it the richest header source for Cluster on Outlook.

### Cited Findings

**Gmail (consumer + Workspace)**
- Google has claimed since 2017 that ML blocks "more than 99.9%" of spam/phishing; the 2017 post said a dedicated model selectively delays "less than 0.05% of messages" for phishing analysis (vendor; secondary) — [BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/google-says-gmail-now-blocks-99-9-percent-of-spam-and-phishing-emails/). Current Workspace whitepaper wording: blocks "more than 99.9% of threats" (vendor) — [Google Workspace security whitepaper](https://workspace.google.com/learn-more/security/security-whitepaper/asset-protection/). The denominator is never stated (inference: 0.1% of Gmail volume is still a very large absolute number, and the figure says nothing about targeted impersonation).
- 2020: Gmail blocked "more than 100 million phishing emails every day" (vendor; secondary) — [same BleepingComputer/Google coverage](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/google-says-gmail-now-blocks-99-9-percent-of-spam-and-phishing-emails/).
- RETVec (Nov 2023): swapping Gmail's spam classifier text vectorizer for RETVec raised spam detection rate over baseline by **38%** and cut false positive rate by **19.4%**, with 83% less TPU use (vendor, verified on Google's blog per search extract) — [Google Security Blog](https://security.googleblog.com/2023/11/improving-text-classification.html). RETVec is built to resist homoglyph/typo/invisible-character text evasion (inference from its design goal; relevant because Cluster's lure lexicon is defeated by exactly these).
- Authentication mandates: after Gmail began requiring some authentication (2022), unauthenticated messages Gmail users receive fell **75%**; after the Feb 2024 bulk-sender rules (>5,000/day to Gmail: SPF+DKIM+DMARC, one-click unsubscribe, spam rate <0.3%), unauthenticated messages fell a further **65%**, "265 billion fewer" in 2024 (vendor via secondary) — [Valimail summary](https://www.valimail.com/?p=9773), [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2024/04/02/gmail_turns_20/). Caveat: many domains that published DMARC to comply stayed at `p=none` (secondary vendor blog) — [AutoSPF](https://autospf.com/blog/google-yahoo-bulk-sender-mandate-changes-over-two-years/).
- Warning tiers (secondary; no official consumer page retrieved): red "This message seems dangerous" / "This message might be dangerous" (higher severity: phishing signals, user reports, auth failure) vs. grey "Be careful with this message" and "Gmail couldn't verify that <domain> actually sent this message" (lower severity) — [Suped](https://www.suped.com/learn/email-deliverability/why-is-gmail-showing-this-message-seems-dangerous-warning), [Technipages](https://www.technipages.com/gmail-this-message-seems-dangerous). Prior note cites Google help 185812 for the consumer page.
- Workspace (admin, paid) "Spoofing and authentication" settings, each defaulting to "Keep email in inbox and show warning": (a) **"Protect against spoofing of employee names"** — sender name matches a name in the Workspace directory but the email isn't from the company domain/aliases; (b) inbound emails spoofing your domain without SPF or DKIM pass; (c) **unauthenticated emails** — must pass SPF or DKIM for *any* domain, otherwise a **question mark** shows next to the sender name — [Google Workspace admin help](https://knowledge.workspace.google.com/admin/gmail/advanced/advanced-phishing-and-malware-protection) (verified via search extract). Conflict: Prelude Security describes the domain-spoof check as applying even when auth passes; Google's own page says SPF/DKIM-unauthenticated — [Prelude](https://www.preludesecurity.com/blog/hardening-google-workspace-email). Directory-name matching is org-only; there is no equivalent for a consumer's Contacts (consistent with the prior note's finding, still absence-of-evidence).
- BIMI/verified checkmark (since May 2023): blue checkmark requires DMARC enforcement (`p=quarantine`/`reject`), BIMI record, SVG Tiny PS logo and a **VMC** (trademark-backed). Google accepted **CMCs** (no trademark) from 2024-09-24 for logo display only; CMC does **not** unlock the checkmark (secondary/vendor) — [9to5Google 2023](https://9to5google.com/2023/05/03/gmail-blue-checkmark/), [Red Sift](https://redsift.com/blog/gmail-expands-bimi-indicators-to-include-a-verified-icon), [Sectigo](https://www.sectigo.com/blog/gmail-blue-checkmark-bimi-requirements). VMC cost ~$750-1,400/yr (single vendor estimate) — [guptadeepak.com](https://guptadeepak.com/guides/gmail-blue-checkmark-bimi/).
- Gemini-era scam detection (Oct 2026): what I found is for **Google Messages / Android / Chrome**, not Gmail. Messages is getting opt-in cloud-Gemini (Private AI Compute) scam detection for unknown-sender conversations; Chrome Enhanced Protection uses on-device Gemini Nano (since May 2025) for tech-support scams — [Droid Life 2026-10-14](https://www.droid-life.com/2026/10/14/google-messages-is-getting-a-massive-gemini-powered-scam-defense-upgrade/), [PhoneArena](https://www.phonearena.com/news/google-just-gave-chrome-android-secret-weapon-against-online-scams_id170233). No source confirmed Gemini-based scam banners inside Gmail.

**Yahoo**
- Yahoo moved yahoo.com DMARC from report to **`p=reject` on 2014-04-04**, asking all receivers to reject unsigned mail claiming a Yahoo From; Yahoo (2014-04-11) said it was "highly effective at reducing spoofed email"; it broke mailing lists (Yahoo users posting to lists bounced); AOL followed 2014-04-22 — [Word to the Wise](https://www.wordtothewise.com/2014/04/yahoo-statement-dmarc-policy), [PCWorld](https://www.pcworld.com/article/444768/yahoo-email-antispoofing-policy-breaks-mailing-lists.html), [The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2014/04/yahoos-new-dmarc-policy-destroys-every.html). Trigger was compromised yahoo.com accounts spamming contacts (secondary).
- Yahoo 2024 sender requirements: enforcement from Feb 2024, List-Unsubscribe from June 2024; non-compliant mail spam-foldered or rejected with a specific error; Yahoo refuses to state a bulk volume threshold — [Yahoo Sender Hub FAQ](https://senders.yahooinc.com/faqs). Microsoft applied similar rules to outlook.com/hotmail.com/live.com from May 2025 (secondary) — [Spotler](https://help.spotler.co.uk/hc/en-gb/articles/16713853218194-Yahoo-Google-2024-Sender-Guidelines).
- Yahoo consumer scam-warning banners and Yahoo BIMI specifics: not found (see Gaps).

**Outlook.com / Microsoft 365** (most header-exposed; verified on Microsoft Learn, updated 2026-08)
- **Unauthenticated sender `?`**: added to sender photo if the message "doesn't pass SPF or DKIM checks **and** doesn't pass DMARC or composite authentication" — [MS Learn anti-phishing policies](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/defender-office-365/anti-phishing-policies-about).
- **"via" tag** (`chris@contoso.com via fabrikam.com`): shown when the From domain differs from the DKIM `d=` domain **or** the MAIL FROM domain (and isn't a subdomain) — [same](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/defender-office-365/anti-phishing-policies-about).
- **First contact safety tip**: shown "the first time they get a message from a sender" or when "they don't often get messages from the sender". Text: "You don't often get email from <address>." Multi-recipient: majority model, "Some people who received this message don't often get email from…". Not stamped for S/MIME-signed mail, SCL -1 bypass, or **mailboxes under 7 days old**. Header marker: `SFTY:9.25` in `X-Forefront-Antispam-Report` — [same](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/defender-office-365/anti-phishing-policies-about). Available in all cloud mailbox plans (EOP), not just Defender.
- **Impersonation (Defender-only, paid)**: user impersonation (max **350** protected senders/policy), domain impersonation (max **50** domains; catches TLD swaps and "even slightly similar" like `contosososo.com`), mailbox intelligence. Crucially: when mailbox intelligence is on, **user and domain impersonation "doesn't work if the sender and recipient previously communicated"**; mailbox intelligence itself only flags senders with no prior communication. Example given: a real vendor also named "Gabriela Laureano" at fabrikam.com is not flagged for recipients with history with that address — [same](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/defender-office-365/anti-phishing-policies-about).
- Safety-tip copy (verbatim, reusable pattern): user impersonation `SFTY:9.20`: "<Sender> appears similar to someone who previously sent you email, but may not be that person." Domain `SFTY:9.19`: "This sender might be impersonating a domain that's associated with your organization." Unusual characters: "The email address <address> includes unexpected letters or numbers. We recommend you don't interact with this message." — [same](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/defender-office-365/anti-phishing-policies-about).
- Microsoft: "a composite authentication failure doesn't directly result in a message being blocked… holistic evaluation" to avoid blocking legit domains with weak auth — [same](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/defender-office-365/anti-phishing-policies-about).
- **"Unverified" sender label** (MC1112452, rolled out mid-July 2025, seen 2025-08-18): tag in message list + reading-pane ribbon when a message "is suspicious but isn't deemed malicious… the sender may not be who they appear to be"; shown **only on the first message in a thread**; no admin control. Seen in Outlook mobile/classic/OWA/new Outlook; Outlook.com consumer not confirmed — [Office365ITPros](https://office365itpros.com/2025/08/19/unverified-sender-outlook/).
- Consumer Outlook.com advanced security (Microsoft 365 Personal/Family): advanced attachment malware scanning, link checking with warning page on click; auto-on for @outlook/@hotmail/@live/@msn; not for connected Gmail/Yahoo accounts — [Microsoft Support](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/office/882d2243-eab9-4545-a58a-b36fee4a46e2). No impersonation/mailbox-intelligence feature is listed for consumers.

**Header formats Cluster can parse on Outlook (verified)** — [MS Learn anti-spam headers](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/defender-office-365/message-headers-eop-mdo):
- `Authentication-Results: … compauth=<pass|fail|softpass|none> reason=<3 digits>`; evaluated on the From domain.
  - `000` explicit fail (DMARC fail with p=quarantine/reject); `001` **implicit fail** (no auth records, or weak policy ~all/?all/p=none); `002` admin-prohibited spoof pair; `010` intra-org spoof.
  - `1xx` pass: `100` SPF or DKIM pass and aligned; `101` DKIM signed by From domain; `102` SPF aligned pass; `103/104` PTR alignment; `108` DKIM broken by legit hop body change; `109` no DMARC but would pass; `111` DMARC temp/perm error but aligned; `112` DNS timeout; `115` From domain is an accepted domain of sending M365 org; `116` MX aligns with PTR; `130` trusted ARC sealer override.
  - `2xx` softpass (`201`/`202` PTR-based); `3xx` not checked; `4xx`/`9xx` bypassed (`905` complex routing); `501/502` valid NDR; `6xx` implicit fail (`601` self-to-self spoof); `7xx` pass (`701-704` "history of receiving legitimate messages from the sending infrastructure").
  - DMARC values include `bestguesspass` (no DMARC record, would have passed); action values `oreject`, `pct.quarantine`, `pct.reject`.
- `X-Forefront-Antispam-Report: …CAT:<…>;SFV:<…>;SFTY:<…>;SCL:<…>;CIP:…;CTRY:…;DIR:INB…` — `CAT` values: `SPOOF`, `PHSH`, `HPHSH`, `SPM`, `HSPM`, `BULK`, `MALW`, plus Defender-only `UIMP` (user impersonation), `DIMP` (domain), `GIMP` (mailbox-intelligence impersonation), `BIMP` (brand). `SFV:SFE` = sender in user's Safe Senders, `SFV:BLK` blocked list, `SFV:NSPM` non-spam, `SFV:SPM` spam. `SFTY:9.19/9.20/9.25` as above. `X-Microsoft-Antispam: BCL:<n>` bulk complaint level. Microsoft: in cloud orgs `SCL` doesn't determine the action; use `CAT` and `DIR`.

**Apple Mail / iCloud, Proton, Fastmail, Tuta**
- Proton Mail red banner: "This email has failed its domain's authentication requirements. It may be spoofed or improperly forwarded!" — [Proton support](https://proton.me/support/email-has-failed-its-domains-authentication-requirements-warning). Proton also shows a phishing banner when its system detects phishing, and an **"Official" badge** on genuine Proton mail (a Proton claim without the badge is likely phishing) — [Proton report-phishing](https://proton.me/es-es/support/report-phishing) (search extract). A researcher reported a sender-spoofing display bypass (capital "I" for "l" lookalike of gmail.com); bounty paid April 2025; reportedly still working Sept 2026 (secondary, unverified) — [CyberInsider](https://cyberinsider.com/?p=344296).
- Apple Mail "trusted sender" label is a **local familiarity signal** (address in Contacts, replied-to, earlier conversations), not a verification; phishing can carry it (secondary) — [Fox News/CyberGuy](https://www.foxnews.com/tech/phishing-scam-exploits-apple-mail-trusted-sender-label).
- Fastmail, Tuta, Proton Sentinel's email-side behaviour: nothing reliable found (Gaps).

### Inferences
- The consumer market splits into two signals: "did authentication pass" (`?`, grey banners, Proton red banner) and "is this a verified brand" (BIMI checkmark, Proton Official badge). Nobody consumer-side answers "is this the person you think it is". That is Cluster's open lane.
- Apple's "trusted sender" label is a cautionary tale: a familiarity badge without an identity check **helps** attackers who reuse a familiar address, display name or thread. Cluster should never show a positive "trusted" badge from familiarity alone.
- On Outlook, Cluster can borrow Microsoft's verdicts for free from headers: `compauth`+`reason`, `CAT`, `SFTY:9.25` (Microsoft's own first-contact judgement over the *full* mailbox, which fixes part of Cluster's 30-day sample problem), and `SFV:SFE` (user's Safe Senders). Gmail exposes far less (Authentication-Results with spf/dkim/dmarc, ARC).
- `compauth=fail reason=001` (implicit fail) is the common case for small/old domains and should be weak; `reason=000` (explicit DMARC fail at enforcement) is near-decisive for a brand claim.

### Gaps
- Official Gmail consumer help text for each banner and the exact trigger logic: support.google.com unreachable from this environment; Google doesn't publish trigger thresholds anyway.
- Whether Outlook.com **consumer** mailboxes stamp `SFTY:9.25`, the `?` and the "Unverified" label: Microsoft documents these for "cloud mailboxes" (Exchange Online). Needs a live look at a consumer Outlook.com message header.
- Yahoo scam banners, Yahoo BIMI behaviour, Apple/iCloud junk logic, Fastmail and Tuta warning features, Proton Sentinel email features: no reliable sources found in this pass.

## 2. Enterprise vendors that made measurable progress

### Takeaway
Every vendor with credible results does the same thing: a **per-org (per-person) history of who sends as whom**, used as a *gate*, then a **second content/intent signal** to decide. The only peer-reviewed numbers (Barracuda BEC-Guard) show the header-identity signal alone is useless for blocking (11.7% precision) and excellent once combined (98.2%). Sublime's public rules are the best concrete catalogue of header-level patterns and the negations that keep them quiet.

### Cited Findings

**Barracuda BEC-Guard / Sentinel** (Cidon et al., USENIX Security 2019; in production since July 2017) — verified, [paper PDF](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/sec19-cidon.pdf), [USENIX page](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity19/presentation/cidon)
- Design target: FP rate 1 in a million emails, precision 95%.
- Two stages: header **impersonation classifier** (random forest) gates a **content classifier** (TF-IDF + KNN text; link classifier). Content runs only on header-flagged mail.
- Header features (Table 3): sender from corp domain?; Reply-To ≠ sender?; **number of times this sender name and email address appeared together**; number of times Reply-To address appeared; Reply-To from a known web service (LinkedIn, Salesforce allowlist)?; **sender name popularity**.
- **Results (Table 6)**, 200M emails, several hundred orgs, 4,221 labelled BEC (June 2018): combined precision **98.2%**, FP **0.000019% (1 in 5,260,000)**, recall **96.9%**. Impersonation classifier **alone**: precision **11.7%**, FP **0.016% (1 in 6,300)**, recall 100%.
- Attack mix (3,000 attacks, Table 1): wire transfer 46.9% (no link), click link 40.1%, establish rapport 12.2% (no link), steal PII 0.8%. **59.9% of attacks had no link.** ~43% impersonated CEO/founder.
- Top BEC phrases by TF-IDF: "got moment", "response", "moment need", "moment", "need", "need complete", "ASAP", "urgent response", "urgent", "complete task".
- Name normalisation: stores <first, last>; matches variants (drop middle name/initial, reversed "Smith, Jane", strip suffixes like "Jr." or an email address embedded in the display name) and **nickname list** (Bill↔William).
- Address diversity: in a 44,000-mailbox org over 3 months, **82% of users sent from only one address**; senders with many addresses were often those repeatedly impersonated, or shared mailboxes ("IT", "HR").
- Warm-up: "the classifier performs well after only one month of data."
- Labelling assumption: an impersonating address is used ≤100 times (spam filter handles volume). Stated limitation: a patient attacker who sends hundreds of benign mails first can "legitimise" an address. Not covered: compromised real accounts with unchanged Reply-To; impersonation of **external** contacts (needs inbound history).

**Microsoft Defender** — see section 1. Its documented design choice: prior two-way communication *suppresses* impersonation verdicts (mailbox intelligence) (verified, [MS Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/defender-office-365/anti-phishing-policies-about)). No FP rate published.

**Abnormal Security** (vendor)
- VendorBase: cross-customer federated vendor database; per-vendor risk score across (a) vendor domain impersonated/spoofed, (b) vendor account appears compromised, (c) vendor looks illegitimate; plus customer reports — [Abnormal VendorBase](https://abnormal.ai/platform/product-demos/vendorbase), [Abnormal VEC use case](https://abnormal.ai/resources/use-case-vendor-email-compromise).
- Worked VEC example: real vendor mail passed SPF/DKIM/DMARC with no links; flagged because none of **1,300+ prior emails** from that vendor came from the Netherlands, **Reply-To changed to a lookalike domain**, and the body was an urgent payment request (vendor) — [Abnormal VEC use case](https://abnormalsecurity.com/resources/use-case-vendor-email-compromise). No independent accuracy evaluation found.

**Sublime Security** (open-source MQL rules; verified by reading rule source on GitHub, [repo](https://github.com/sublime-security/sublime-rules/tree/main/detection-rules)). Header-heavy rules, with the logic that matters for Cluster:
- `vip_impersonation.yml` (high): display name **exactly equals** a protected name, or starts with it and is longer ("John Doe CEO") **only with high-confidence BEC intent**; AND sender is new/outlier and unsolicited, or has prior malicious/no benign history, or has a Reply-To not equal to the sender and not previously "solicited". Negates: personal↔work self mail (sender display name == mailbox display name with empty To/Cc), postmaster/mailer-daemon, rfc822/delivery-status/calendar attachments.
- `impersonation_employee_payroll_fraud.yml` (high): display name contains a space (skips "Billing") and matches an org display name; sender not org domain or is freemail; no attachments, <10 links, body <800 chars; payroll/direct deposit/ACH/salary regex; unsolicited; never benign; **negates real reply threads** (References/In-Reply-To present with previous thread).
- `impersonation_employee_suspicious_sender.yml`: freemail sender + display name matches org name + local part contains mail/office/staff/executive + subject equals the recipient's first name.
- `impersonation_free_email_masquerade_vip.yml` (high): freemail + local part `^c(hair|eo)\d+`.
- `sender_freemail_replyto_different_freemail.yml` (medium): From is freemail, Reply-To is a **different** freemail (excl. googlegroups), with list-forwarder negations (riseup X-Original-From, secureserver bounces).
- `headers_freemail_replyto_returnpath_mismatch.yml` (high): From not freemail but Reply-To or Return-Path is; high-confidence BEC/advance-fee intent; negates Gmail autoforward `+caf_=`, list mail ("via" + List-Unsubscribe), and **any message with References/In-Reply-To**.
- `impersonation_fake_msg_thread_mismatched_from_freemail_replyto.yml`: new/outlier unsolicited sender + freemail Reply-To ≠ From + "RE:/FWD:" subject or ≥3 of from:/to:/sent:/date:/cc:/subject: in body + **References empty or In-Reply-To null** (fake thread). Excludes support/sales/noreply/marketing local parts and list headers.
- `fake_thread_suspicious_indicators.yml`: same fake-thread test (reply/forward subject without References/In-Reply-To), unsolicited, and **4 of** several content signals (request+financial, invoice, urgency…). Negates bounces, Google Calendar return-path, Mimecast internal.
- `vendor_impersonation_thread_hijack.yml` (high): reply subject; sender root domain **not in known sender domains** and never in previous-thread senders/recipients; Levenshtein **1-2** from a known sender domain; BEC intent; financial topic; WHOIS not found or previous thread quotes the sender's display name; sender domain new or known <3 days.
- `lookalike_sender_domain.yml` (high): SLD >3 chars; Levenshtein == 1 to an org domain; sender new/outlier and unsolicited or never benign; trusted-domain negation unless DMARC fails.
- `sender_new_from_domain_first_time_sender.yml`: WHOIS age ≤10 days and unsolicited. `headers_replyto_new_domain_nlu_request.yml`: Reply-To root ≠ sender root and Reply-To domain ≤30 days old (or sender <30 days on suspicious TLD) + request + financial/urgency.
- `recipients_undisclosed_compauth_check.yml`: undisclosed/empty To and no Cc/Bcc, **2 of** {compauth not pass/softpass, high-confidence BEC/cred-theft intent, phishing link}, new/outlier sender.
- `sender_suspicious_display_name_from_gmail_domain.yml` (low): gmail.com sender, no links/attachments, display name has **2 of** ~30 cue words (kindly, urgent, asap, verify, respond, mobile, text, request, weekday/month names…).
- `impersonation_microsoft.yml` (high): display name in an exact list ("Microsoft", "Microsoft account team", "Microsoft Support", "Microsoft 365 Message center"…), confusable-letter regex over "Microsoft", or body cues; **negated only if root domain is in a corporate list (microsoft.com, office.com, microsoftonline.us, skype.com, microsoftstoreemail.com…) AND DMARC passes**. **outlook.com / hotmail.com are not in that list**, so "Microsoft account team" from outlook.com fires. Brand rules use `strings.replace_confusables()` and `ilevenshtein(display_name, brand) <= 1`.
- Recurring negation pattern across nearly every rule: `not (sender root in $high_trust_sender_root_domains and DMARC pass)`, `profile.by_sender().solicited` (recipient has written to the sender), `any_messages_benign`, and list/bounce/calendar exclusions.
- No published FP rates for any Sublime rule (Gaps).

**Conversation hijacking** (Barracuda, early 2020, vendor): domain-impersonation attacks used in conversation hijacking rose from ~500 (July 2019) to 2,000+ (Nov 2019) out of ~500k monthly attacks analysed; attackers usually send from **lookalike domains** rather than the compromised account so the attack outlives account cleanup (secondary) — [KnowBe4](https://blog.knowbe4.com/domain-impersonation-increases-by-400-as-part-of-conversational-hijacking-attacks), [TechTarget](https://www.techtarget.com/healthtechsecurity/news/366595844/Evasive-Domain-Impersonation-Phishing-Attacks-Increase-by-400).

**Proofpoint, Mimecast, Ironscales, Tessian, Material Security**: not researched in depth in this pass; no measured FP figures found (Gaps).

**Scale of the problem (FBI IC3, secondary)**: BEC 2024: 21,442 complaints, ~$2.77-2.8B losses (2nd by losses, >17% of $16.6B total) — [Nacha](https://www.nacha.org/news/fbis-ic3-finds-almost-85-billion-lost-business-email-compromise-last-three-years). 2025: 24,768 complaints, ~$3.046B (≈+10%), total cyber-enabled losses ~$20.9B (one source says $17.6B; conflict) — [McDonald Hopkins](https://www.mcdonaldhopkins.com/insights/news/the-sobering-truth-of-the-fbis-2025-internet-crime-complaint-center-report), [Insurance Business](https://www.insurancebusinessmag.com/us/news/cyber/business-email-fraud-drives-bulk-of-us-cybercrime-losses-analysis-590687.aspx). Not checked against ic3.gov.

### Inferences
- BEC-Guard's 11.7% → 98.2% jump is the strongest published evidence that a contact-impersonation signal must be a gate, not a verdict. At 1 FP in 6,300 messages, a header-only name check on a ~3,200 msg/month household would raise a false alarm roughly every two months; with an independent second signal it drops to effectively never (inference from Table 6 rates applied to Cluster's mailbox size; household base rates differ from enterprise).
- BEC-Guard's 82% single-address figure (enterprise *employees*) vs. Ho's 52% multi-address figure (all established *From names*) are not in conflict: employees use one corporate address; the outside world (friends, family, services) does not. A household inbox looks more like Ho's population, so name-to-address changes will be common and must not alert alone.
- The "solicited" concept (has the recipient ever written to this address/Reply-To?) is Sublime's main FP brake and maps directly to Cluster's sent-mail `knownSenderSet`. Sublime also applies it to the **Reply-To** address separately (`beta.profile.by_reply_to().solicited`), which Cluster's ledger could copy cheaply.
- Microsoft's "prior communication suppresses impersonation" rule protects against FPs but blinds the system to compromised real accounts; Cluster's ledger (DKIM/Reply-To baseline changes on a *known* sender) is the complementary piece.
- Fix for gap A5 (brand claim from the brand's own freemail): follow Sublime's pattern exactly. Allowlist = the brand's **corporate sending root domains with DMARC pass**, not "domains the brand owns". outlook.com/hotmail.com/live.com, gmail.com/googlemail.com and icloud.com/me.com/mac.com are consumer freemail for this purpose; a "Microsoft account team" display name from them should count as a decisive freemail-brand claim.

### Gaps
- No public FP rates for Sublime, Abnormal, Microsoft impersonation, Proofpoint, Mimecast, Ironscales, Tessian or Material.
- Tessian "misdirected email" and Material Security specifics not retrieved.
- Primary IC3 report PDFs not read.

## 3. Academic work on warnings and explanation UX

### Takeaway
Warnings work when they **interrupt** and sit **at the point of action** (the link or the reply), and they fail when passive or frequent. Habituation is fast, so the warning budget must be tiny and the wording specific. False-positive warnings still get obeyed a lot, so each FP costs real trust and real friction.

### Cited Findings
- **Egelman, Cranor & Hong, CHI 2008**: 97% of 60 participants fell for at least one spear-phish; **active** (interruptive) browser warnings were heeded **79%** of the time; the passive warning was heeded by only one participant and was not significantly different from no warning — [CMU KiltHub](https://kilthub.cmu.edu/articles/journal_contribution/You_ve_Been_Warned_An_Empirical_Study_of_the_Effectiveness_of_Web_Browser_Phishing_Warnings/6626570), [PDF](https://css.csail.mit.edu/6.566/2018/readings/youve-been-warned.pdf).
- **Petelka, Zou & Schaub, CHI 2019** (n=701, MTurk, ~100/condition; verified from paper text) — [PDF](https://yixinzou.github.io/publications/pdf/chi2019-petelka.pdf), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1145/3290605.3300748). Phishing click-through rate (CTR): no warning **76.67%**; **email banner 44.00%**; static link-focused 33.65%; on-hover 30.36%; static + forced attention 15.64%; browser interstitial 11.78%; **on-hover + forced attention 8.67%** (Kruskal-Wallis χ²(6)=180, p<.001). Forced attention = original link deactivated; user must click the unmasked URL inside the warning. Heading tested best as "Fake Website, Don't Click!" (85% correctly identified the hazard). The paper notes even a 1.35% FP-rate classifier would produce >1.8M false positives/day at internet scale.
- **Petelka, Berens, Sugatan, Volkamer & Schaub, IEEE S&P 2025** (n=1,320; verified from paper text) — [PDF](https://jpetelka.github.io/files/ieee_2025_phishing.pdf): control phishing CTR **87.5%** vs **43.9%** with any warning; focused attention 38.5% vs 49.2% without; time delay 42.2% vs 50.5%; both combined **37.5%**; 5-second delay + focused attention 37.6%. False-positive warnings (warning on a benign link): users still clicked through only 32.8% (focused attention) / 41.4% (time delay), i.e. users **obey false alarms** most of the time. Older participants clicked less. Brand-keyword lookalike URLs fooled more (CTR 44.5-46.1%) than gibberish URLs (41.0%); one participant trusted `westernunionpay.com` because it contained "Western Union" and "https".
- **Vance, Kirwan, Bjornn, Jenkins & Anderson** (CHI 2017; MIS Quarterly 2018): fMRI + eye tracking over five days: attention to warnings drops across the week, partly recovers overnight; **polymorphic** warnings (only four visual variations) resisted habituation substantially better; a 3-week field study showed the same; authors recommend limiting how many warnings are shown — [MISQ](https://misq.org/tuning-out-security-warnings-a-longitudinal-examination-of-habituation-through-fmri-eye-tracking-and-field-experiments.html), [Cambridge SHB slides](https://www.cl.cam.ac.uk/events/shb/2017/vance1.pdf), [BYU](https://scholarsarchive.byu.edu/facpub/9293).
- Microsoft's reason copy (section 1) is the industry's clearest identity-specific explanation: names the comparison ("appears similar to someone who previously sent you email") and the uncertainty ("may not be that person").

### Inferences
- A top-of-message banner is the weakest effective format (44% CTR vs 77% none). Cluster runs in Gmail/Outlook web, so it can attach the warning where the action is: next to links (and deactivate them on held mail), and on the **Reply** button for contact-impersonation/BEC (the risky action in no-link BEC, ~60% of attacks per BEC-Guard, is replying or paying, not clicking).
- Because users obey ~60-67% of false warnings, every FP trains them to do the wrong thing on the next real one and costs friction on legit mail. That supports Cluster's strict hold gate, and a separate, quieter "note" tier for single weak signals that never interrupts.
- Habituation argues for: few warnings, varying the visual by reason type (a light form of polymorphism comes for free if each reason has its own icon and line), and never warning on the same sender repeatedly (Microsoft's "Unverified" label shows only on the first message in a thread).
- Reason text should name the specific mismatch and the safe action out-of-band: "This uses Lisa's name, but it isn't the address Lisa uses. If it's about money, call her on a number you already have." Proton and Microsoft both point users to a known channel.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed field study of Gmail's own banners' effectiveness found.
- No study of reason-code wording for **contact** impersonation specifically (all studies are link/URL-centred).
- No just-in-time training data specific to consumer email found in this pass.

## 4. Account-takeover, vendor fraud and thread hijack: header signals

### Takeaway
Compromised accounts and hijacked threads are best caught by **change against a sender's own history** (new Reply-To, new signing domain, new sending infrastructure) plus **thread-integrity checks** (a "Re:" with no References/In-Reply-To; a reply whose sender domain never appeared in the thread and is one edit from a domain that did). All are cheap from headers. All need a payment/urgency content signal before acting.

### Cited Findings
- BEC-Guard treats "Reply-To ≠ sender" and "times this Reply-To has appeared" as core features, with an allowlist of services that legitimately set Reply-To (LinkedIn, Salesforce); it lists "attacker sends from the real address without changing Reply-To" as uncovered — [BEC-Guard](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/sec19-cidon.pdf).
- Abnormal's VEC example used **new geography over 1,300 prior emails + Reply-To changed to a lookalike + urgent payment** — [Abnormal](https://abnormalsecurity.com/resources/use-case-vendor-email-compromise) (vendor).
- Fake-thread detection from headers: subject is RE:/FW: (or quoted header block in body) **and** `References` empty or `In-Reply-To` missing — [Sublime fake_thread_suspicious_indicators](https://github.com/sublime-security/sublime-rules/blob/main/detection-rules/fake_thread_suspicious_indicators.yml), [Sublime fake msg thread + freemail Reply-To](https://github.com/sublime-security/sublime-rules/blob/main/detection-rules/impersonation_fake_msg_thread_mismatched_from_freemail_replyto.yml).
- Genuine-thread negation: many Sublime BEC rules **skip** messages with References/In-Reply-To present (real replies) — [Sublime payroll rule](https://github.com/sublime-security/sublime-rules/blob/main/detection-rules/impersonation_employee_payroll_fraud.yml), [freemail Reply-To/Return-Path rule](https://github.com/sublime-security/sublime-rules/blob/main/detection-rules/headers_freemail_replyto_returnpath_mismatch.yml).
- Real-thread hijack by lookalike: reply from a domain never seen in the thread and Levenshtein 1-2 from a known sender domain, domain known <3 days — [Sublime vendor_impersonation_thread_hijack](https://github.com/sublime-security/sublime-rules/blob/main/detection-rules/vendor_impersonation_thread_hijack.yml). Barracuda: hijackers prefer lookalike domains over the compromised account (vendor) — [KnowBe4](https://blog.knowbe4.com/domain-impersonation-increases-by-400-as-part-of-conversational-hijacking-attacks).
- Microsoft `compauth` reasons `701-704` ("history of receiving legitimate messages from the sending infrastructure") show Microsoft itself uses sender-infrastructure history as a trust input — [MS Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/defender-office-365/message-headers-eop-mdo).
- Patient attackers can legitimise an address with hundreds of benign mails first (BEC-Guard limitation) — [BEC-Guard](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/sec19-cidon.pdf).

### Inferences (header-only signals Cluster could add, ranked by value/cost)
1. **In-Reply-To/References → own Sent mail check.** If a message claims to reply to a thread (RE: subject or In-Reply-To), resolve the referenced Message-ID against the user's mailbox. Missing/unknown references on a "RE:" from a non-correspondent = fake thread. If the reference resolves but the thread's prior participants never included this sender's root domain, and the domain is ≤2 edits from one that was, = lookalike hijack (strong). Gmail/Graph APIs allow Message-ID lookup (inference: quota cost is one search per candidate).
2. **Reply-To "solicited" baseline.** Track whether the user has ever *sent to* the Reply-To address, separately from the From address. Unsolicited Reply-To + freemail or ≤30-day-new domain + payment language = strong.
3. **Freemail ↔ freemail Reply-To mismatch** (From gmail.com, Reply-To outlook.com) and **non-freemail From with freemail Reply-To/Return-Path**. Pure header checks; Sublime ships them at medium/high severity with list/autoforward negations (`+caf_=`, List-Unsubscribe + "via", X-Original-From).
4. **Infrastructure change on a known sender.** Cluster's ledger already tracks DKIM d= and Reply-To. Adding Return-Path root domain and, on Outlook, `CTRY`/`CIP` from `X-Forefront-Antispam-Report` would replicate Abnormal's "first email from the Netherlands in 1,300" signal. Gmail exposes Received headers only (inference: parsing the first external hop is brittle).
5. **Payment-change lexicon in subject** (bank details, new account, ACH, wire, direct deposit, invoice update, gift card) as the second signal. BEC-Guard's top phrases ("got a moment", "urgent", "ASAP", "complete task") cover the rapport opener.

### Gaps
- No published FP rates for thread-integrity checks; legit causes of missing References (some mobile clients, forwarding, mailing lists, calendar systems) are known only from rule negations.
- No academic study measuring consumer (household) vendor-fraud base rates.

## 5. Combining weak signals with tiny false positives for one household mailbox

### Takeaway
The proven recipe has three parts. (1) An identity gate from per-mailbox history (name-and-address co-occurrence, solicited, first-seen). (2) A **second, independent** signal: content intent, a freemail/new-domain/Reply-To anomaly, or auth failure at enforcement. (3) Aggressive **negations** for known-legit patterns (real threads, lists, auto-forwarding, trusted domains with DMARC pass, self-mail). Cluster's existing "score ≥6 AND (2 kinds OR decisive)" rule matches this; what's missing is the contact identity gate and the negation set.

### Cited Findings
- Header identity alone: 11.7% precision; header + content: 98.2% precision, 1 in 5.26M FP — [BEC-Guard Table 6](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/sec19-cidon.pdf).
- Mailbox-intelligence suppression: prior communication disables impersonation verdicts — [MS Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/defender-office-365/anti-phishing-policies-about).
- First-contact tip suppressed for mailboxes <7 days old (a cold-start guard) — [MS Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/defender-office-365/anti-phishing-policies-about). BEC-Guard: good after one month of history — [BEC-Guard](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/sec19-cidon.pdf).
- Sublime uses "N of M" combinators (2 of / 4 of) and `solicited`/`any_messages_benign` negations; exact-match on protected names, with fuzzy/prefix matches allowed **only** with high-confidence BEC intent — [vip_impersonation.yml](https://github.com/sublime-security/sublime-rules/blob/main/detection-rules/vip_impersonation.yml), [fake_thread_suspicious_indicators.yml](https://github.com/sublime-security/sublime-rules/blob/main/detection-rules/fake_thread_suspicious_indicators.yml).
- Display name must contain a space before matching a person (avoids "Billing", "Payments") — [payroll rule](https://github.com/sublime-security/sublime-rules/blob/main/detection-rules/impersonation_employee_payroll_fraud.yml). Self-mail negation (sender display name == mailbox owner's name) — [vip_impersonation.yml](https://github.com/sublime-security/sublime-rules/blob/main/detection-rules/vip_impersonation.yml).
- Microsoft: compauth failure alone doesn't block; holistic evaluation avoids hurting legit weak-auth domains — [MS Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/defender-office-365/anti-phishing-policies-about).
- Users obey most false warnings (FP CTR 32.8-41.4%, i.e. 59-67% heed) — [Petelka 2025](https://jpetelka.github.io/files/ieee_2025_phishing.pdf). Habituation is fast; limit warnings — [Vance et al.](https://misq.org/tuning-out-security-warnings-a-longitudinal-examination-of-habituation-through-fmri-eye-tracking-and-field-experiments.html).

### Inferences: a concrete header-only design for Cluster (all inference)
**Contact-impersonation gate (new, closes A4):**
- Build a *name index* from (a) display names on the user's **Sent** To/Cc (names the user typed or their client resolved) and (b) display names of ledger-familiar senders. Normalise like BEC-Guard: lowercase, NFKC, `replace_confusables`, strip middle names/initials, reverse "Last, First", strip suffixes and embedded addresses, nickname table.
- Fire `contact-name-new-address` when the normalised display name equals an indexed contact's name (require ≥2 tokens, i.e. a space) **and** the From address has never been seen with that name **and** the user has never sent to it (unsolicited).
- Suppress when: the new address's root domain equals the contact's known root domain (BEC-Guard/Ho multi-address legit case at the same org); the message is a real reply (In-Reply-To resolves to a message the user sent); sender name equals the mailbox owner's own name (self-mail between personal accounts); List-Id/List-Unsubscribe present; Reply-To is a known service (LinkedIn etc.).
- Never decisive. Hold only with a second kind: payment/gift-card/urgency subject, freemail From when the contact normally uses a non-freemail domain, unsolicited or freemail Reply-To, risky attachment, auth failure, or ≤30-day domain.

**Signal weights (proposal):**
- Decisive: brand display name (exact/confusable/≤1 edit) from consumer freemail incl. the brand's own (outlook.com, gmail.com, icloud.com), with DMARC pass only on the brand's corporate roots counting as legit; `compauth=fail reason=000` on a brand claim; known-bad domain list; lookalike (≤1 edit) of a domain the user has *corresponded with* (not just the 37 brands), domain first seen <3 days, on a reply.
- Strong: fake thread (RE: + no References/In-Reply-To + unsolicited); Reply-To freemail with non-freemail From; freemail↔different freemail Reply-To; Reply-To unsolicited and different root from From; identity-change on known sender (existing ledger).
- Weak (note-only alone): contact-name-new-address; first contact (or Outlook `SFTY:9.25`); `compauth=fail reason=001`; `?`/via conditions; lure subject; display-name cue words (Sublime's 2-of list).

**Negation set to add (from Sublime/BEC-Guard):** Gmail auto-forward `+caf_=` in Return-Path; List-Unsubscribe + " via " in display name; Google Calendar bounce return-path; delivery-status/rfc822 attachments; known Reply-To services; trusted root + DMARC pass; mailbox <7 days of history for first-contact.

**Reason-code UX:**
- One line per reason, naming the mismatch: "Uses Lisa's name, but not an address Lisa has written from." / "Says it's Microsoft, but it's from a personal outlook.com account." / "Looks like a reply, but it isn't part of any conversation you had." / "Replies would go to a different address (…@gmail.com) than the sender."
- One safe action: "Check with them another way before paying or sharing details."
- Put the warning at the action: disable links on held mail with a forced-attention reveal of the real URL (8.67-15.64% CTR vs 44% for banners); attach the contact-impersonation note to Reply.
- Show at most one interruptive warning per sender/thread; vary icon per reason type.

### Gaps
- No published household-scale FP baselines. Cluster needs its own local measurement (e.g. count how often contact names appear with new addresses in the owner's full history, per the prior note's "data still missing" table).
- Name-collision rate in a family mailbox (two different "John Smith"s, or relatives sharing a surname plus first initial) is unmeasured; Microsoft's Gabriela Laureano example shows it happens.
