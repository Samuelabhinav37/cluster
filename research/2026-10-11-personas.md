# Cluster personas: who has the problems, why, and how Cluster solves each one

Date: 2026-10-11. Builds on the owner's four personas (student, multi-store owner, 80-year-old,
creator), the candidates in `2026-10-06-personas-phishing-and-web-security.md` (caregiver, job
seeker, home buyer, newcomer, high-risk) and today's problem research
(`2026-10-10-customer-problems-and-product-map.md`, `…-why-email-problems-happen…`,
`…-research-findings-and-solutions.md`).

Personas are **composites**: names, ages and inbox numbers are illustrative. Every pain is tied to a
cited finding. Problem codes P1–P10 are from the product-map note (P1 too much mail, P2 missing what
matters, P3 scams, P4 unsubscribe, P5 storage, P6 trust, P7 hard tools, P8 finding later, P9
several accounts, P10 time).

## 1. Maya, 20, university student

**Inbox:** personal Gmail plus a university address forwarded into it. ~60 emails a day: clubs,
university lists, every shop she has bought from, food delivery, apps she signed up for once.
Thousands unread. Applying for internships.

**Why she has these problems**
- Every checkout and app sign-up adds her to a list (P1); personal inboxes are "type-overloaded" with
  many kinds of mail mixed together (Grevet et al., CHI 2014).
- Job scams target students: the FTC warned in April 2024 about fake recruiters, spoofed professor
  emails and fake-cheque scams aimed at students ([News4Jax on the FTC alert](https://www.news4jax.com/money/2024/04/10/job-scams-are-targeting-college-students-too-ftc-says)).
  In CMU's 515-person study, **18–25-year-olds were the most vulnerable** ([School of Phish](https://kilthub.cmu.edu/articles/journal_contribution/School_of_Phish_A_Real-World_Evaluation_of_Anti-Phishing_Training/6625691)).
- She needs mail from strangers (recruiters), so blocking all new senders would hurt her.

**What she wants:** a clean inbox in minutes, not hours; never miss an interview email; not get
scammed.

**What she fears:** deleting something from her university or an employer.

**How Cluster solves it**
| Need | Cluster feature | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Clear thousands of promos fast | Clear out suggestion cards ("Never opened · 41 senders · 2,300 emails") | Redesign |
| Stop new ones | Verified unsubscribe; promotions sorted out of the inbox for 1 day | New / built |
| Never lose uni or job mail | Protect `.edu` and senders she replies to; job mail never held by New senders | Partly |
| Fake recruiters | Brand-claim-from-free-mail rule ("Amazon Recruiting" &lt;x@gmail.com&gt;), job-lure subjects, first contact → hold with one reason | Partly (brand list) |
| Learn safely | Teaching card when she releases a held scam | New |

**Setup answer:** "I'm a student" → promotions 1 day, newsletters 3 days, scam holding on, New
senders **off**, job-scam rules on.

**Success for Maya:** inbox from strangers' shops drops; no held recruiter email turns out to be real
(release rate on job holds stays low).

## 2. Raj, 44, owns three shops

**Inbox:** Google Workspace for the shops, Outlook for the accountant and bank, personal Gmail.
Invoices, supplier orders, delivery confirmations, customer complaints, marketplace notices. Checks
mail between customers, often on his phone.

**Why he has these problems**
- Business email compromise is the second-costliest cybercrime: **$2.77 billion in 2024** (FBI IC3,
  via [Proofpoint](https://www.proofpoint.com/us/blog/email-and-cloud-threats/email-attacks-drive-record-cybercrime-losses-2024)).
  "Invoice inquiry" is the most common vendor-fraud pretext, **41.3%** of high-risk campaigns (Abnormal,
  via [Abnormal](https://abnormal.ai/ja/blog/2026-attack-landscape-report-vec)) (Vendor).
- These emails often have **no link or attachment**, so link scanners miss them.
- Most domains can be spoofed (~84% no DMARC, Red Sift), including small suppliers.
- He uses several accounts across Google and Microsoft (P9).

**What he wants:** never pay a fake invoice; never miss a real one; spend less time in email.

**What he fears:** a supplier's "new bank details" email that looks exactly right.

**How Cluster solves it**
| Need | Cluster feature | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Catch fake suppliers | **Pinned contacts**: he pins his suppliers' domains; any lookalike (edit distance ≤ 2) or "updated bank details" from a first-time sender is held with "This looks like Harlow Supplies, but the address is different." | New |
| Catch Reply-To tricks | Reply-To differs from From on a payment subject → high risk | Partly |
| See all accounts | Multiple accounts with one switcher; Home total across accounts | New (accounts work) |
| Find invoices fast | Important strip: invoices and bills due | New |
| Less noise | Marketplace and supplier newsletters sorted out | Built |

**Setup answer:** "I run a business" → scam holding on, pinned contacts prompt, invoices protected,
promotions 1 day.

**Success for Raj:** zero payments to held senders; pinned suppliers' real mail is never held.

## 3. Margaret, 79, retired, and 4. Lisa, 49, her daughter

**Margaret's inbox:** one Gmail she has had for 15 years. Grandchildren's photos, bank and pension
letters, church newsletters, and a steady stream of "your account is suspended" emails. She won't open
a dashboard; she reads mail in Gmail on a tablet.

**Lisa** set up the tablet, lives two hours away, and worries.

**Why they have these problems**
- People 60+ reported **$2.4 billion** in fraud losses in 2024, up from about $600 million in 2020; the
  FTC estimates the real cost at **$10.1–81.5 billion** ([FTC, Dec 2025](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/12/ftc-issues-annual-report-congress-agencys-actions-protect-older-adults)).
  In FBI data, phishing/spoofing is the most common complaint for 60+ (`2026-10-06` note).
- Older users are **less aware of their own risk** (Oliveira, Lin et al.); a quiet warning badge is
  ignored, an interrupting one is heeded by 79% (Egelman et al., CHI 2008).
- **86%** of adults looking after both teens and ageing parents say managing their online safety adds
  stress (Cox survey via [NBC San Diego](https://www.nbcsandiego.com/nbc-7-responds-2/sandwich-generation-scams-targeting-their-kids-parents/3886497/?amp=1)).
  AARP's advice to adult children: set up the parent's email so known contacts come first
  ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/well/2022/07/29/how-to-protect-parents-from-scams-and-financial-fraud/)).

**What Margaret wants:** to know if an email is real, without learning an app.
**What Lisa wants:** to set it up once and trust it.
**What they fear:** Margaret, being tricked; Lisa, not being there when it happens.

**How Cluster solves it**
| Need | Cluster feature | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Scams never reach her inbox | Scam holding **on by default**; held mail goes to "🚨 Possible phishing" in Gmail, so the warning travels with the message | Built |
| No dashboard needed | **Simple mode**: one Home screen, large text; everything else works in Gmail itself | Built |
| Known people first | New senders held only when they claim to be a bank, government or family emergency; grandchildren and contacts always through | Partly |
| Lisa sets it up once | **Family setup** path: Lisa runs setup on Margaret's Chrome, picks "Set up for a parent", strict defaults locked behind a simple PIN | New |
| Lisa can check | Gmail delegation lets Lisa see the held label from her own phone (no Cluster server needed) | Documented, not built |
| Teach without lecturing | One teaching card if Margaret releases a held scam | New |

**Constraint to design around:** Cluster is a desktop Chrome extension; it can't run on Margaret's
tablet. Setup happens on a computer (hers or Lisa's, signed in as Margaret). What then keeps working
from the tablet: Gmail filters Cluster created (sorting, mute, certain-scam rules) run inside Gmail
with no computer on; labels and held mail show in the Gmail app. What needs that computer's Chrome to
be open now and then: the 1-minute scam check and the clean-up sweeps. The family setup screen must
say this plainly.

**Success:** no losses; Margaret never has to open Cluster; Lisa checks the held label weekly.

## 5. Leo, 27, creator

**Inbox:** a public business email on his channel page, plus personal Gmail. 30–80 pitches a week:
real brands, agencies, fake sponsors, "collab" spam, platform notices.

**Why he has these problems**
- Attackers posed as sponsors offering demos of VPNs, games and antivirus tools; the "demo" was
  cookie-stealing malware that hijacked YouTube channels, later sold for **$3 to $4,000** or used for
  crypto scams. Google disrupted it from 2019; it used **1,011 domains** and about 15,000 accounts
  (Google TAG, via [Dark Reading](https://www.darkreading.com/attacks-breaches/google-phishing-campaign-targets-youtube-creators),
  [Security Affairs](https://securityaffairs.com/123630/hacking/youtube-creators-accounts-hijacked-malware.html)).
- His address is public, so he can't hide it, and he needs mail from strangers (brands).
- Two accounts (P9).

**What he wants:** see real deals quickly; never open malware; keep brand mail and fan mail apart.

**What he fears:** losing his channel to one download.

**How Cluster solves it**
| Need | Cluster feature | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Spot fake sponsors | First contact + "sponsorship/collab/brand deal" subject + risky attachment (zip, rar, scr, lnk, iso) or download-link language → hold with "First email from this sender, with a file type often used for malware." | Partly (risky-attachment signal built) |
| Verify brands | Brand-claim check: "Nord" in the name from a domain that isn't Nord's → warn | Partly |
| Real deals stand out | Pitches sorted into "📨 Pitches" label instead of mixed into the inbox | New kind |
| Two accounts | Multiple accounts | New |

**Setup answer:** "I'm a creator" → New senders off, pitch sorting on, attachment checks strict.

**Success for Leo:** no malware attachments opened from held senders; pitches findable in one label.

## 6. Dana, 38, working parent

**Inbox:** personal Gmail shared with the family's Google account for photos; two school apps that
also email; sports clubs; bills; deliveries; work on a separate account.

**Why she has these problems**
- **56%** of parents get too many school emails, **52%** feel overwhelmed by their personal inbox,
  **62%** have missed an important event or detail ([Scary Mommy](https://www.scarymommy.com/parenting/parent-email-study));
  almost a third say school emails are more stressful than their work inbox
  ([Motherly](https://www.mother.ly/news/parents-school-email-survey)).
- Schools without one platform can make families use **10–15 apps** (Cornerstone/Edsby 2025, via
  [Kids on the Yard](https://kidsontheyard.com/en/article/parent-teacher-collaboration)) (secondary).
- Gmail, Drive and Photos share 15 GB; when full, Gmail stops sending and receiving
  ([Google One Help](https://support.google.com/googleone/answer/6374270)).

**What she wants:** never miss a school deadline or bill; get storage back without losing photos;
fewer interruptions.

**What she fears:** deleting something the school or bank sent.

**How Cluster solves it**
| Need | Cluster feature | Status |
|---|---|---|
| School mail never missed | **Important strip**: school senders (learned from her replies and known school-platform domains), "permission slip", "due", "deadline" subjects | New |
| Bills and deliveries | Important strip: bills due, deliveries today | New |
| Storage full | **Storage meter**: "Promotions with attachments use 2.1 GB" → one-click Trash; receipts and school mail protected | New |
| Fewer interruptions | **Delivery windows**: promotions and updates arrive at 9am, 1pm, 6pm (batching 3×/day lowered stress in two studies) | New |
| Less noise | Promotions sorted out, verified unsubscribe | Built / new |

**Setup answer:** "I'm busy / a parent" → Important strip on, delivery windows on, promotions 1 day.

**Success for Dana:** no missed school deadlines she has to tell us about (opt-in "Did Cluster help?"
prompt); storage below 80%.

## How we'll check the personas are right (no server, no tracking)

1. **Five-person hallway test per persona** before building each feature: show the mockup, ask them
   to do the persona's main task, watch where they stop. (Inference; standard usability practice.)
2. **Opt-in, on-device prompts** after real moments: "Was this really a scam?" when releasing,
   "Did this help?" after a clean-up. Answers stay on the device and tune that person's settings.
3. **Owner's own mailbox and family** as the first live test for Margaret/Lisa and Dana.
